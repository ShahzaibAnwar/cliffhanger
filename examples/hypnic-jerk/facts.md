# Facts — hypnic jerk (Part 1)

| claim in the reel | wording used | confidence | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypnic jerks are sudden involuntary twitches at sleep onset, often with a falling sensation | "you fall… your whole body jerks awake" | high | Sleep Foundation, "Hypnic Jerks" · Cleveland Clinic, "Hypnic Jerk" |
| Very common: 60–70% of people experience them | "Up to seventy percent", labelled *commonly cited estimate* | medium (survey-based) | Sleep Foundation; Cleveland Clinic |
| Leading explanation: the brain misreads muscle relaxation at sleep onset as falling | "your brainstem… reads that sudden drop as a fall" | medium (hypothesis) | Cleveland Clinic; review literature on sleep starts |
| Arboreal-primate evolutionary theory | "One theory… our ancestors slept in trees", chip *THEORY · NOT PROVEN* | low–medium (speculative) | Coolidge & Wynn hypothesis (2009), widely reported |
| More frequent with stress, caffeine/stimulants, sleep deprivation | "stressed, wired on caffeine, or exhausted" | high | Sleep Foundation; Cleveland Clinic |
| Exploding head syndrome: hearing a loud bang at sleep onset/waking; harmless; cause unclear | "a gunshot inside their head… real, harmless, nobody fully knows why" | high | Sharpless (2014) review, *Sleep Medicine Reviews*; Cleveland Clinic |

Dramatized delivery, not data: "your brain thinks you're falling. For real." is framed as the
mechanism hypothesis, not a claim of danger.
