## LinkedIn
Your brain runs a safety system that would rather be wrong than late.

As you fall asleep, your muscles relax quickly. The brainstem, still on watch, can read that sudden drop as a fall and fire a reflex through every muscle at once: the hypnic jerk. Up to 70% of people experience it (a commonly cited estimate).

One evolutionary theory says the reflex protected primate ancestors who slept in trees. Whether or not that's the origin, it's a neat example of a design principle we use in engineering too: a fast false positive costs far less than a missed real event.

It also gets worse with stress, caffeine and sleep deprivation, which is worth knowing if you're running on all three this quarter.
